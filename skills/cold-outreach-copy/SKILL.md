# Cold Outreach Copy

## When to use
- Writing a cold email to a prospect who never opted in.
- Writing a LinkedIn connection note (max 300 chars) or an InMail.
- Building a 3 to 5 touch follow-up sequence that ladders value across touches.
- Deciding which single angle and which one hook to lead with for a specific lead.
- Reviewing an outbound draft for deliverability, grounding, or IL compliance.

Do NOT use for: warm/inbound replies, nurture to opted-in lists (that is lifecycle email), or social comments and DM replies on others' content (use `social-engagement`). For cold WhatsApp/Telegram as a first touch: blocked in Israel, do not draft it.

## Operative rules (always-on; mirrors the registry entry)
1. STRATEGY FIRST. Pick the SINGLE strongest angle: the why-now / reason-to-believe most relevant to THIS prospect given the facts you actually have. Add at most ONE personalization hook. Better no hook than a forced one.
2. STRUCTURE SHORT. Open with specific relevance, never "I hope this finds you well" or "hope this email finds you well". One line of value tied to their situation. One clear low-friction ask (a question, or a 15-minute call). Done. No walls of text, no feature lists.
3. GROUNDING. Never fabricate a fact about the lead. Lean only on provided facts. No creepy over-personalization (nothing that signals stalking, e.g. their kid's school, home address, a screenshot of their private activity).
4. DELIVERABILITY. Avoid spam triggers: ALL-CAPS, "!!!", "free"/"guarantee" stacks, too many links. Plain, human text.
5. COMPLIANCE (IL). Cold WhatsApp/Telegram as a first touch is blocked; use email or LinkedIn for cold. Honor opt-out immediately. One ask, one message; sequence value across follow-ups instead of stuffing one message.

## Deep reference (the on-demand layer)

### 1. Pre-qualification gate (run BEFORE writing a word)
Outreach to a bad-fit lead is wasted inventory and hurts sender reputation. Score fit first; only write if it clears the bar. Distilled from `sales-strategist` (MEDDIC/BANT/SPICED).

| Framework | Use when | Gate: write outreach only if... |
|-----------|----------|--------------------------------|
| BANT | High-volume, transactional (< 25K ACV) | There is a plausible Need and the person has (or routes to) Authority. Budget/Timeline can be discovered later. |
| MEDDIC | Complex / enterprise | You can name a likely Metric (pain worth money) and a plausible Champion or Economic Buyer. Skip if you cannot articulate the pain. |
| SPICED | Consultative | You can state a Situation and a Pain the offer credibly touches. |

Minimum bar to send: (a) a real, evidenced reason THIS lead has the problem you solve, and (b) the recipient can act or forward. If you cannot state the pain in one sentence, do not send. A confident-but-unqualified send is worse than an obvious skip.

ICP check: firmographics (size, industry, geo), fit-to-offer, buying signal. Below threshold on fit means do not spend the touch, no matter how good the copy.

Anti-pattern from `sales-strategist`: "happy ears" (assuming fit), "demo-first" (pitching before you understand the pain), single-threaded blasting.

### 2. Angle-selection decision table
Pick ONE. The angle is the reason THIS message exists now.

| Signal you actually have | Angle to lead with | Opening move |
|--------------------------|--------------------|--------------|
| A trigger event (funding, hire, launch, new location, role change) | Why-now / timing | Name the event, then the problem it creates. |
| A visible pain on their site/product/reviews | Problem-Agitate (PAS, light) | State the specific gap you observed, factually. |
| A peer/competitor already solved this with you | Social proof / unity | "Teams like [peer] use us for X" (only if true). |
| They wrote/spoke publicly on the topic | Liking + relevance | React to the actual point they made, add one insight. |
| Nothing specific, but strong ICP fit | Value-first / reciprocity | Lead with a concrete useful idea or teardown, no ask attached yet. |
| No real signal and weak fit | Do not send | Route back to pre-qualification gate. |

Rule: never combine two angles in one message. If two are tempting, the second becomes the theme of follow-up touch 2 (ladder, do not stack).

### 3. Cold-email skeleton (with per-line word budget)
Total target: 50 to 90 words in the body. Under 125 words always. On mobile the whole thing should fit one screen without scrolling.

| Line | Purpose | Word budget | Rules |
|------|---------|-------------|-------|
| Subject | Earn the open | 3 to 6 words | Lowercase or sentence case, specific, no clickbait, no "!". See formulas below. |
| Open (1 line) | Specific relevance / the hook | 8 to 18 words | Prove you sent this to THEM, not 500 people. Never "hope this finds you well". |
| Value (1 to 2 lines) | One benefit tied to their situation | 15 to 30 words | Their outcome, not your feature list. One specific number if you truly have one. |
| Ask (1 line) | One low-friction next step | 8 to 15 words | A question or a 15-min call. Interest-based ask ("worth a look?") beats a hard calendar demand on touch 1. |
| Sign-off | Human close | 2 to 5 words | Real name. No "Warm regards, The Growth Team". |

Optional: a single short P.S. can carry the ONE proof point (social proof or a relevant result). Max one link in touch 1.

### 4. Subject-line formulas + length
Keep to 3 to 6 words, under ~45 characters so it survives mobile truncation. Lower-case reads more human/1-to-1.

| Formula | Example (EN) | Why it works |
|---------|--------------|--------------|
| {their company} + {topic} | "acme onboarding drop-off" | Signals relevance, not a blast. |
| Question about their situation | "who owns SDR reporting there?" | Triggers curiosity + a reply reflex. |
| Trigger event reference | "congrats on the Tel Aviv office" | Timely, human. |
| Peer/result reference | "how {peer} cut CAC 20%" | Social proof (only if real). |
| Ultra-plain internal-looking | "quick question" | High open rate but weak qualification; use sparingly. |

Never: ALL-CAPS, "FREE", "GUARANTEED", "!!!", "Re:" fakery, emoji-stuffing.

### 5. Personalization-hook quality rubric (good vs creepy)
One hook, grounded, or none.

| Good (relevant, public, professional) | Creepy / forced (never) |
|---------------------------------------|--------------------------|
| A point from their public post/podcast/talk | Anything about their family, kids, home |
| A company trigger: funding, hire, launch, new location | "I saw you were online at 11pm" |
| A visible gap on their site/product/reviews | A private detail that signals surveillance |
| Their role's known pain, stated generally | A fake "I loved your work!" with no specifics |
| A named mutual connection or shared group | Guessing a pain you have no evidence for |

Test: could you say this hook to their face in a hallway without it being weird? If no, cut it. If the only hook you can find is forced, drop the hook and lead on angle + ICP relevance instead. Better no hook than a fake one.

### 6. Spam-trigger blacklist (deliverability)
Plain, human text lands in the inbox. Avoid:

| Category | Examples to avoid |
|----------|-------------------|
| Formatting | ALL-CAPS words, "!!!", colored/huge fonts, lots of bold |
| Money words stacked | "FREE", "100% free", "guarantee", "risk-free", "no cost", "cash", "$$$" |
| Hype | "act now", "limited time", "once in a lifetime", "amazing", "incredible" |
| Links/images | More than 1 to 2 links, link shorteners, image-only emails, tracking-heavy |
| Structure | Long walls of text, many recipients, no plain-text version, spammy sign-offs |
| Tech | Mismatched from-name/domain, no unsubscribe on bulk, unauthenticated domain (set SPF/DKIM/DMARC) |

One idea, one ask, one link. Write like a person emailing one person.

### 7. Follow-up sequence (3 to 5 touches, value laddered)
One ask per message. Each touch adds NEW value; never "just bumping this" with nothing new. Space touches 3 to 5 business days apart. Stop on reply or opt-out.

| Touch | Day | Channel | New value it adds | Ask |
|-------|-----|---------|-------------------|-----|
| 1 | 0 | Email or LinkedIn | The angle + one hook | Soft interest ("worth a look?") |
| 2 | ~4 | Same | A proof point: a peer result or a mini insight not in touch 1 | Same ask, reframed |
| 3 | ~9 | Same or switch channel | A resource: a relevant teardown, template, or 1-line idea (reciprocity) | 15-min call |
| 4 | ~16 | Same | A different angle (the one you parked in the table) | One specific question |
| 5 (break-up) | ~23 | Same | Permission-to-close: "should I close the loop?" | Yes/no reply, easy out |

Ladder principle (from `persuasion-principles` email sequence): touch 1 relevance, touch 2 social proof, touch 3 reciprocity/value, touch 4 new angle, touch 5 graceful exit. Never stuff all of this into one message.

### 8. Cialdini principle to outreach-tactic mapping
Ethical use only: persuasion helps a good-fit prospect decide, it never tricks a bad-fit one. From `persuasion-principles`.

| Principle | Cold-outreach tactic | Guardrail |
|-----------|----------------------|-----------|
| Reciprocity | Lead with a free useful idea/teardown before any ask | Must be genuinely useful, not a disguised pitch |
| Social proof | "Teams like {named peer} use us for X"; a specific number | Only real, verifiable, from a similar company |
| Authority | One relevant credential or result in the P.S. or signature | Relevant to their problem, not vanity |
| Liking | React to their actual public point; find real common ground | No fake flattery; must be specific |
| Commitment/consistency | A tiny first ask (a question, a yes/no) before the call | Do not manufacture fake prior commitments |
| Scarcity | Rare in cold outreach; only real capacity limits ("we take 3 clients/quarter") | Never fake deadlines; fake scarcity kills trust and deliverability |
| Unity | "As a fellow {Israeli SMB founder / developer}..." shared identity | Must be true shared identity, not a costume |

Default cold stack: Liking (relevance) -> Reciprocity (value) -> light Social proof (P.S.) -> small Commitment (the ask). Skip Scarcity on first touch.

### 9. Copy frameworks for the value line (from `brand-copywriter`)
The body is too short for a full framework, but the value line borrows one:

| Framework | Shape | Best cold-outreach fit |
|-----------|-------|------------------------|
| PAS | Problem, Agitate, Solution | You have evidence of a real, felt pain. Keep Agitate to half a sentence. |
| BAB | Before, After, Bridge | You can paint a concrete better-state outcome. |
| FAB | Feature, Advantage, Benefit | Rarely; only when the mechanism itself is the hook. Lead with Benefit. |

Voice rules carried over: write to one person, active voice, contractions, specific numbers ("cut CAC 20%" not "significantly"), benefits over features, one clear CTA that names what happens. Banned tells: "game-changer", "seamless", "leverage", "unlock", "revolutionary", "let's dive in", "It's not X. It's Y.", three-fragment punch lines, a closing one-liner that restates the message. No em-dash.

### 10. LinkedIn: connection note vs InMail
| | Connection note | InMail |
|--|-----------------|--------|
| Length | 300 chars HARD cap (aim 200 to 250) | Longer allowed; still keep it short, treat like a cold email |
| Subject | None | Yes: use the subject formulas above |
| Best use | Open a relationship; NO pitch | Direct ask allowed but still lead with relevance |
| Rule | One reason to connect + zero ask, or one tiny ask | One angle, one ask, same as email |
| Tone | Peer-to-peer, human, specific | Peer-to-peer; never a wall of text |

Connection-note pattern: {specific relevance / shared context} + {one line of why connecting is useful to THEM}. Do not sell in the note; earn the accept, then have the conversation. Never send a pitch immediately after they accept ("connect-and-pitch" is the most-blocked pattern).

### 11. IL compliance specifics (חוק הספאם / Communications Law amendment 40)
- Cold WhatsApp and Telegram as a FIRST touch are blocked. Email and LinkedIn are the cold channels. WhatsApp only after opt-in or an existing relationship.
- Honor opt-out immediately and permanently; every bulk email needs a working unsubscribe.
- One ask per message; do not stuff. Sequence value across follow-ups.
- No fabricated stats, prices, or fake urgency (also a HELIX brand rule).
- Hebrew must read as native Israeli, RTL, ₪ before the number (₪400). Do not machine-translate English outreach into Hebrew; write it native.
- Never name or criticize a competitor in the copy (name the better way, not the rival).

### 12. Worked before/after examples

Example A, English cold email.

BEFORE (bad):
> Subject: AMAZING opportunity for Acme!!!
> Hi there, I hope this email finds you well! We are a WORLD-CLASS, game-changing platform that helps companies UNLOCK growth. We offer a FREE, no-risk, 100% guaranteed trial. Click here [link] and here [link] and here [link] to learn more. Would love to hop on a call this week to walk you through all our features!

Problems: ALL-CAPS, "!!!", "free/guarantee" stack, "hope this finds you well", three links, feature-dump, generic, no real relevance, hard ask.

AFTER (good):
> Subject: acme trial-to-paid drop
>
> Saw Acme just opened self-serve signup. Usually when that ships, trial-to-paid conversion is the first thing that leaks.
>
> We helped Riverline lift theirs 18% by fixing the first-run flow, no new features.
>
> Worth a quick look at your funnel? Happy to send two specific ideas either way.
>
> Ron

Why: one angle (trigger + pain), one grounded hook (they shipped self-serve), one real proof point, one low-friction ask, plain text, one channel.

Example B, native Hebrew cold email (Israeli SMB, לא תרגום).

BEFORE (bad):
> נושא: הצעה מדהימה!!! חינם לחלוטין
> שלום רב, אני מקווה שהמייל מוצא אותך בטוב. אנחנו פלטפורמה עולמית פורצת דרך שתעזור לך לפרוץ קדימה. מציעים ניסיון חינם ללא סיכון עם התחייבות מלאה. לחץ כאן [קישור] וכאן [קישור] כדי לגלות את כל הפיצ'רים שלנו. אשמח לקבוע שיחה השבוע.

AFTER (good):
> נושא: הלידים שנופלים אצלכם בטופס
>
> ראיתי שפתחתם סניף חדש בחיפה, מזל טוב. בדרך כלל כשמוסיפים סניף, הטופס באתר מתחיל לאבד פניות כי הוא לא מותאם לנייד.
>
> לעסק דומה שלנו סידרנו את זה והפניות עלו בכ-15%, בלי לבנות שום דבר חדש.
>
> שווה שאשלח לך שתי נקודות ספציפיות שראיתי אצלכם? בלי התחייבות.
>
> רון

Why: relevance (סניף חדש), one grounded hook, one concrete proof (₪-free but a real number), one soft ask, dugri and native, no em-dash, no banned hype, ₪ convention respected, one channel (email, not WhatsApp).

Example C, LinkedIn connection note (under 300 chars):
> ראיתי את הפוסט שלך על גיוס SDRים בלי תקציב ענק, הנקודה על הכשרה פנימית מדויקת. אני עובד עם עסקים ישראליים על בדיוק זה. אשמח להתחבר ולהחליף רעיונות.

No ask, no pitch, specific reference, peer-to-peer.

## Anti-patterns / common mistakes
- Two angles or a feature list in one message. Pick one; ladder the rest.
- "Hope this finds you well", "Just following up", "circling back" with nothing new.
- Forced personalization ("I loved your work!") with no specific. Cut the hook instead.
- Fake scarcity or fake deadlines in cold outreach. Kills trust and deliverability.
- Connect-and-pitch on LinkedIn: pitching the second they accept.
- Cold WhatsApp/Telegram first touch (illegal in IL) or ignoring opt-out.
- Fabricated stats, prices, or peer names. If it is not a real, verifiable fact, do not state it.
- Machine-translated Hebrew. Write native or do not write Hebrew.
- More than one link, ALL-CAPS, "!!!", "free/guarantee" stacks.
- Writing before qualifying. No stated pain in one sentence means no send.

## Checklist before returning
- [ ] Lead qualified: I can state the pain and why THIS lead has it in one sentence.
- [ ] Exactly ONE angle; the rest are parked for follow-ups.
- [ ] At most ONE hook, grounded in a provided/public fact (or no hook, on purpose).
- [ ] Body under ~125 words; opens with specific relevance, not "hope this finds you well".
- [ ] Exactly one low-friction ask; one link max.
- [ ] Zero fabricated facts, stats, prices, or peer names.
- [ ] No spam triggers: no ALL-CAPS, "!!!", "free/guarantee" stack, link pile.
- [ ] Channel legal for cold in IL (email/LinkedIn, not WhatsApp/Telegram first touch); opt-out honored.
- [ ] Hebrew (if any) reads native Israeli, RTL, ₪ before number.
- [ ] No em-dash. No banned AI tells. No competitor named. One human sign-off.
- [ ] Follow-ups (if drafted) ladder NEW value, one ask each, stop on reply/opt-out.
