# Social Engagement

Writing comments and DM replies in a brand's name on other people's content, plus creating brand social posts. This is the deep on-demand layer behind the short registry entry `social-engagement`. Load it when you need decision tables, worked examples, or platform detail; the registry entry alone carries the operative rules an agent applies at runtime.

## When to use

- Drafting a comment on someone else's post as the brand (a reaction, an answer, an add to the discussion).
- Drafting a DM reply in the brand's name (an inbound question, a thank-you, a lead follow-up).
- Creating an original brand post (LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, X) or a reshare with a take.
- Deciding whether a post is safe to engage at all, or should be flagged to a human or skipped.
- Reviewing autopilot-generated engagement before it goes public.

Not for: cold outbound first-touch (use `cold-outreach-copy`), paid ad copy (use `paid-ads`), long-form content strategy (use `seo-geo-pack` or a content skill), or community architecture from scratch (use the `community-building` skill).

## Operative rules (always-on; mirrors the registry entry)

1. **Read first.** Understand the post before replying. Choose an angle and tone that fit the specific context, not a generic reaction.
2. **Sensitive posts: FLAG (tread carefully) or SKIP.** Grief, loss, complaints, crisis, politics, medical. Never sell on those. If a human presence is warranted, keep it human and brief, or hand to a person.
3. **Brand-safe.** On-brand human voice that adds value (insight, a real answer, a genuine reaction). Not salesy. Not a template. No spam. No ToS-violating patterns (mass-identical comments, link-dropping).
4. **DM discipline.** Never over-promise. Never make an unauthorized commitment on pricing, delivery, or refunds. Never claim an action was taken. Answer helpfully, or hand off to a human for anything binding.
5. **Gating.** On autopilot, anything that fails brand-safety downgrades to human-approval. When unsure, hold. Keep a reversibility mindset: a posted comment is public and hard to unsay.

Plus the HELIX house rules that apply to every output: no em-dash, never name or criticize a competitor in public text, no fabricated stats or prices, Hebrew must read native (not translated), one CTA maximum and only when appropriate (most engagement carries no CTA at all).

## Deep reference (the on-demand layer)

### 1. Sensitive-post detection table

Scan the post for these signals before drafting. Match the strongest signal, then take the action.

| Signal in the post | Read | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Death, funeral, "passed away", memorial, "in loving memory" | Grief / loss | SKIP (or a human posts a one-line condolence, never the brand-as-brand, never a CTA) |
| Illness, diagnosis, hospital, treatment, mental-health struggle | Medical | SKIP or FLAG to human. No advice, no product mention |
| Layoff, "open to work", business failure, "we're shutting down" | Loss / vulnerability | FLAG. A warm human line is fine; selling is not |
| Angry review, "worst experience", tagging the brand or a rival in a complaint | Complaint / crisis | FLAG to human owner. Do not defend or argue publicly; acknowledge and move to DM |
| Elections, war, protest, religion, contested policy | Politics | SKIP. The brand takes no side |
| Active outage / scandal about the brand itself | Crisis | FLAG immediately, do not free-hand. Use the approved crisis line |
| Sarcasm, bait, or a thread already turning into a pile-on | Trap | SKIP. Engagement adds fuel, not value |
| Genuine question in the brand's domain | Opportunity | ENGAGE with a real answer |
| Someone sharing a win, a build, a lesson, a useful resource | Opportunity | ENGAGE with a specific reaction that adds something |
| Neutral industry news / trend | Opportunity | ENGAGE with a point of view, no pitch |

Default when the signal is ambiguous: treat as sensitive and HOLD. The cost of an inappropriate public comment is far higher than the cost of staying silent.

### 2. Comment / reply quality rubric (adds value vs template/spam)

A comment ships only if it clears the left column. Anything in the right column is a rewrite or a discard.

| Adds value (ship) | Template / spam (kill) |
|---|---|
| Answers the actual question or extends the actual point | "Great post!" / "So true!" / "Love this 🔥" |
| Adds a specific fact, example, number, or contrasting angle | Generic praise with no content |
| Sounds like one named human wrote it for this one post | Could be pasted under any post in the feed |
| A real reaction (curiosity, disagreement-with-nuance, a relevant story) | A pitch wearing a reaction's clothes |
| Zero or one link, and only when the link genuinely answers the question | Link-drop with a one-word intro |
| Reads native in the poster's language, correct register | Auto-translated, stiff, or wrong register |
| Leaves the poster looking good (builds them up) | Hijacks their post to talk about the brand |

Three quality moves that reliably lift a comment (from social-content's "quality comments"): share a related experience, ask a thoughtful follow-up question, or respectfully disagree with nuance. Never open with the brand; open with the poster's idea.

Mass-identical rule: if the same comment text would be posted under more than one post, it is spam by definition and a ToS risk. Vary or skip.

### 3. STEPPS applied to what earns engagement

Berger's STEPPS (Social currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical value, Stories) explains why a comment or post gets replies, shares, and dwell instead of scrolling past. Use it as a multiplier, not a checklist.

| STEPPS driver | In a comment | In a brand post |
|---|---|---|
| **Social currency** | Give the poster (or a reader) something that makes them look smart for engaging: an insider fact, a "most people don't know" angle | Share a remarkable, quotable stat or a non-obvious insight readers will want to repeat |
| **Triggers** | Tie your reply to a recurring moment the reader hits often ("every time a client asks for X...") so it stays top-of-mind | Anchor the post to a weekly ritual or a common workflow moment |
| **Emotion** | High-arousal beats low-arousal: awe, genuine excitement, useful surprise. Never engineered outrage | Lead with a specific, high-arousal hook. Awe and amusement travel furthest |
| **Public** | Reactions are visible social proof; a substantive comment invites others in | Design for observable output (a carousel, a result screenshot, a shareable line) |
| **Practical value** | The single strongest comment type: a genuinely useful answer people bookmark | Package as a tip / list / how-to the reader would forward to one specific person |
| **Stories** | A short relevant anecdote lands harder than an assertion | Wrap the point in a retellable narrative where the brand is integral, not bolted on |

Two Berger cautions that map directly onto the sensitive-post rules: do not hijack sensitive contexts (grief, health) as triggers, and do not manufacture outrage for reach. Both corrode trust and both are already SKIP territory here.

Practical-value note from `contagious`: narrow beats broad. A comment aimed at "the person who runs Shopify returns" gets forwarded more than one aimed at "everyone".

### 4. Platform matrix

| Platform | Tone | Length | Hashtags | ToS / gotchas |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | Professional, constructive, expertise-first | Comments 1-3 sentences; posts sweet spot 1,300-1,600 chars, first line under ~210 before "See more" | A few, relevant, at the end | Feed favors real expertise and meaningful discussion; penalizes engagement-bait, "pod"-style coordination, and inauthentic mass engagement. External links in the post body suppress reach (put links in a comment) |
| **Instagram** | Warm, visual, personable | Short comments; captions front-load the hook (first ~125 chars show) | Fine to use a cluster; keep on-topic | Comments are heavily anti-spam filtered; identical or link-heavy comments get shadow-limited or flagged. Emoji fits the culture but do not over-brand |
| **Facebook** | Conversational, community, local | Short and human | Rarely needed; not a discovery driver | Groups have their own rules; self-promo without permission gets removed. Brand-as-Page commenting on personal posts can read as intrusive, be judicious |
| **X (Twitter)** | Fast, dry, opinionated, concise | Very short; a sharp reply beats a paragraph | 0-2 max | Fast pile-on culture; a misread reply escalates in minutes. Automation/bulk-reply rules are strict; identical replies trip spam detection |

Cross-platform: the same idea is re-voiced per platform, never copy-pasted. Most brand engagement carries no CTA; when a next step is truly warranted, one specific CTA, once.

### 5. DM escalation / handoff decision tree

```
Inbound DM
│
├─ Is it a simple, factual, already-public answer? (hours, "do you do X?", where-to-find)
│     └─ YES → answer helpfully, warm, no over-promising. Done.
│
├─ Does it ask for a price / quote / discount / refund / delivery date / SLA?
│     └─ YES → do NOT commit. "Let me get you the exact answer" → hand to a human owner.
│
├─ Does it ask us to DO something (cancel, change an order, escalate a complaint)?
│     └─ YES → never claim it's done. Acknowledge, set expectation, route to the human/system that can act.
│
├─ Is it a complaint / upset / public-blowup risk?
│     └─ YES → empathize in one line, move it out of public view, FLAG to a human owner fast.
│
├─ Is it sensitive (grief, medical, legal, financial hardship)?
│     └─ YES → human, gentle, no product. Hand off.
│
└─ Unsure? → HOLD and route to a human. Silence is reversible; a wrong commitment is not.
```

The three DM lines that must never appear from the agent unprompted: a price, a promise of when/what will be delivered, and "done / I've handled that". All three are binding and all three belong to a human.

### 6. Autopilot gating flow

```
Draft (comment / reply / post)
│
├─ Sensitive-post signal? ──yes──▶ downgrade to HUMAN APPROVAL (or SKIP)
├─ Fails quality rubric (template/spam/salesy)? ──yes──▶ rewrite; if still failing, HOLD
├─ Contains a price/commitment/"action taken"? ──yes──▶ HUMAN APPROVAL (binding)
├─ Names or knocks a competitor / uses an unbacked stat? ──yes──▶ block, fix, re-check
├─ Identical to another queued comment? ──yes──▶ vary or drop (ToS)
│
└─ All clear + low-stakes + reversible ──▶ AUTO-POST
                                    │
                                    └─ log it; a public comment is still public, keep the audit trail
```

Rule of thumb: auto-post only clears low-stakes, reversible, brand-safe engagement. Money, commitments, sensitivity, and crisis always route to a human. When the confidence is not high, the default is HOLD, not send.

### 7. LinkedIn post skeleton + hook formulas

Skeleton (short feed post, the workhorse format):

```
[HOOK]        line 1, under ~210 chars, the whole message must survive the "See more" cut
[white space]
[CONTEXT]     1-2 short lines: the situation or the tension
[PAYLOAD]     the insight / story / list, one idea per line, generous line breaks
[TAKEAWAY]    the one thing to remember
[SOFT CTA]    optional, one, specific ("what would you add?" beats "DM me")
[hashtags]    a few, relevant, at the very end
```

Hook formulas (from `social-content`, adapt to brand voice, keep it honest):

- **Value:** "How to [outcome] without [common pain]:" · "[N] things that [outcome]:" · "Stop [mistake]. Do this instead:"
- **Curiosity:** "I was wrong about [belief]." · "The real reason [outcome] happens isn't what you think."
- **Story:** "Last week [unexpected thing] happened." · "3 years ago [past state]. Today [current state]."
- **Contrarian:** "Unpopular opinion: [bold, defensible statement]" · "[Common advice] is wrong, here's why:"

Form note: match CTA and length to the form. A 5-line hot take, a document/carousel deck, and a long-form Article are not interchangeable. Documents and polls tend to over-index on reach; pair either with one clear takeaway.

### 8. Community engagement-ritual ideas

Recurring rituals give a brand reasons to show up in others' feeds without pitching (adapted from `community-building`). Move members up the ladder (1% create, 9% engage, 90% lurk); do not force everyone to post.

| Cadence | Ritual | What the brand does |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Member / customer spotlight | Comment on and reshare a customer's win, crediting them |
| Weekly | "Question of the week" | Post a real question, then reply substantively to every answer |
| Weekly | Tips / how-to | One genuinely useful, forwardable tip (practical value) |
| Daily | Engage-first routine | Reply to every comment on own posts, then add value on 5-10 target-account posts |
| Ad hoc | Recognition | Public shoutout to a helpful community member |
| Monthly | Recap / digest | Round up the best community moments; celebrate contributors |

Onboarding-touch principle that transfers to engagement: react to people like a human (a specific reply to their intro or their post), not a bot. Anti-patterns to avoid: forced engagement, auto-DM blasts, vanity-metric chasing, and never replying to your own commenters.

### 9. Worked before/after examples

**A. LinkedIn comment on a founder's "we just shipped X" post**

- Before (template/spam): "Congrats! 🎉 Amazing work, keep it up! 👏"
- After (adds value): "The part that stands out is that you shipped the messy v1 before the polish. We see the opposite fail all the time: teams polish for months and learn nothing. Curious, did anything in the first week of real usage surprise you?"

Why it works: specific reaction + a contrarian truth (social currency) + a genuine follow-up question, zero pitch, builds the poster up.

**B. Instagram DM, inbound price question (DM discipline)**

- Before (over-commits): "Yes! It's ₪400/month and we can start you this week 😊"
- After (safe): "Good question, glad it caught your eye. I want to give you the exact number for your setup rather than a rough one, so I'm pulling in the person who handles that. They'll come back to you here shortly."

Why it works: no unauthorized price, no delivery promise, warm, routes to a human.

**C. Complaint on Facebook, brand tagged**

- Before (argues in public): "That's not accurate, our support replied to you twice."
- After (de-escalates): "Thanks for flagging this, and sorry it landed this way. I don't want to sort it out over comments where I can't see your account. I've sent you a DM so we can look at exactly what happened."

Why it works: acknowledges, no public defense, moves to DM, flags to a human owner.

**D. Hebrew LinkedIn comment (native, not translated)** on a post where someone shares that their small business finally automated invoicing:

> יפה. הרוב מנסים לאוטמט הכל ביום אחד ונשברים. דווקא לתפוס נקודת כאב אחת, חשבוניות, ולסגור אותה עד הסוף, זה מה שמחזיק. מה הדבר הבא שאתה חושב לקחת?

(Native register, dugri, specific, a real follow-up question, no pitch, no em-dash.)

**E. Hebrew brand post skeleton (native)**, a short useful post, no CTA hard-sell:

> לקוח שאל אותי אתמול למה הצ'אט בוט שלו עונה יפה אבל לא סוגר פניות.
>
> בדקנו, וזה כמעט תמיד אותו דבר: הבוט עונה, אבל אף אחד לא מחליט מתי הוא מפסיק לענות ומעביר לבן אדם.
>
> שלושה מקומות שבהם חייבים להעביר לבן אדם:
> מחיר, מועד אספקה, וכל דבר שמתחיל ב"תבטלו לי".
>
> בוט טוב הוא לא בוט שעונה על הכל. הוא בוט שיודע מתי לשתוק.

(Reads native, practical value, one idea per line, a quotable last line, zero em-dash, no fabricated numbers.)

## Anti-patterns / common mistakes

- Selling on a grief, loss, complaint, crisis, politics, or medical post. Always wrong.
- "Great post!" and other content-free praise. Adds nothing, reads as a bot.
- The same comment copy-pasted under many posts. ToS violation and obvious spam.
- Link-dropping as a comment. Kills reach and trust.
- A DM that quotes a price, promises a delivery date, or says "done". All three are binding and belong to a human.
- Defending the brand or arguing with a complainer in public comments. Move to DM, flag a human.
- Hijacking someone's post to talk about the brand instead of building the poster up.
- Naming or knocking a competitor. Describe the better way, never the rival.
- Auto-translated Hebrew, wrong register, or an em-dash anywhere.
- Auto-posting anything sensitive, binding, or high-stakes instead of downgrading to human approval.
- Engineered outrage for reach, or hijacking a sensitive moment as a "trigger".

## Checklist before returning

- [ ] Read the post; the reply fits this specific context, not a generic slot.
- [ ] Ran the sensitive-post table; grief/loss/complaint/crisis/politics/medical → flagged or skipped, never sold to.
- [ ] Clears the quality rubric: adds value, sounds like one human, not a template, not salesy.
- [ ] Not identical to any other queued comment; zero or one link, and the link earns its place.
- [ ] No DM price/commitment/"action taken"; anything binding is routed to a human.
- [ ] Autopilot gating applied: unsafe → human approval; unsure → HOLD.
- [ ] House rules: no em-dash, no competitor named, no fabricated stat/price, at most one specific CTA (often none).
- [ ] Hebrew (if any) reads native, correct register, RTL, ₪ before the number.
- [ ] Reversibility check: comfortable with this being permanently public under the brand's name.
